DEFINITIONS & CONSTRAINTS:

1. MONOGLOSS: Bare assertions with no acknowledgment of alternative viewpoints. Extract the main verb or verb phrase (including linking verbs like 'is', 'are' and passive forms like 'is illustrated') of a definitive, undeniable factual statement. STRICT RULE: Only tag the verb that carries the assertion — do NOT tag the entire clause.

2. DENY: Rejects an alternative directly. Extract ONLY literal syntactic negators ('no', 'not', 'never', 'none', 'fail') and their immediate concepts (e.g., 'is not located', 'do not have'). STRICT RULE: Do NOT tag negative-sounding words (e.g., 'problem', 'bad') or morphological negation (e.g., 'unable', 'unnecessary').

3. COUNTER: Replaces or contrasts with an expected alternative. Extract single transition words (e.g., 'however', 'although', 'only', 'but', 'yet', 'while', 'whereas') or full contrastive clauses. Multiple COUNTER spans in one sentence are valid if multiple contrastive signals appear.

4. PROCLAIM: Shows strong rhetorical commitment or authorial backing. Extract words and phrases signalling high certainty (e.g., 'In fact', 'undoubtedly', 'indeed', 'the fact', 'conclude', 'demonstrate', 'confirms', 'clearly', 'of course').

5. ENTERTAIN: Presents a proposition as one possibility among others, opening dialogic space. Extract:
   - Modal verbs: 'may', 'might', 'could', 'would', 'will' (when expressing possibility or conditionality), 'shall', 'must' (when epistemic)
   - Evidential markers: 'appear', 'seem', 'suggest' (and their inflected forms)
   - Adverbial hedges: 'probably', 'perhaps', 'apparently', 'generally', 'largely', 'often', 'On average'
   - Conditional words: 'if' (when introducing conditionality)
   - Epistemic phrases: 'I think', 'it is possible that', 'it is likely that'
   STRICT RULE: Tag only the hedge or modal — NOT the full clause it governs.

6. ATTRIBUTION: Delegates responsibility for a claim to an external voice. Extract the reporting verb phrase (e.g., 'strongly asserts', 'reveals', 'stipulate', 'considers', 'emphasize', 'warns') or attributing phrase ('According to', 'as mentioned in', 'as reported by'). Tag ATTRIBUTION independently even when co-occurring with SOURCES.

7. CITATION: Explicit in-text citations identifying a specific source. Extract the ENTIRE citation block including surrounding punctuation and internal spaces exactly as they appear in the sentence (e.g., 'Chomsky ( 1995 )', '[ 10 ]', '( Chan et al 2006 )'). Preserve unusual spacing exactly.

8. SOURCES: The specific named entity or pronoun making a claim. Extract the exact noun or noun phrase (e.g., 'Chomsky', 'researchers', 'Some studies', 'Dow Jones', 'His', 'the Scandinavian energy'). Tag SOURCES independently even when adjacent to ATTRIBUTION or CITATION — they are always separate spans.

9. ENDOPHORIC: Structural references to other parts of the same text. Extract explicit document cross-references including the preposition if present (e.g., 'in Figure 1', 'Table 8', '( 11 )', 'All above', '( references )'). Preserve unusual spacing exactly.

10. JUSTIFYING: Signals persuasion or substantiation — provides reasons, evidence, or causal explanation. Extract causal clause introducers ('because', 'since', 'in that', 'due to') together with their dependent clause, or concluding transition words ('Thus', 'Therefore', 'Hence') with their clause.
