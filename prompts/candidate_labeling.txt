3. COUNTER: Replaces or contrasts with an expected alternative. Extract single transition words (e.g., 'however', 'although', 'only', 'but', 'yet', 'while', 'whereas') or full contrastive clauses.

4. PROCLAIM: Shows strong rhetorical commitment or authorial backing. Extract words and phrases signalling high certainty (e.g., 'In fact', 'undoubtedly', 'indeed', 'the fact', 'conclude', 'demonstrate', 'confirms', 'clearly', 'of course').

5. ENTERTAIN: Presents a proposition as one possibility among others, opening dialogic space.
Extract:

   - Adverbial hedges: 'probably', 'perhaps', 'apparently', 'generally', 'largely', 'often', 'On average'

   - Epistemic phrases: 'I think', 'it is possible that', 'it is likely that'
   STRICT RULE: Tag only the hedge or modal — NOT the full clause it governs.

6. ATTRIBUTION: Delegates responsibility for a claim to an external voice.
Extract the reporting verb phrase (e.g., 'strongly asserts', 'reveals', 'stipulate', 'considers', 'emphasize', 'warns') or attributing phrase ('According to', 'as mentioned in', 'as reported by').
Tag ATTRIBUTION independently even when co-occurring with SOURCES.

7. CITATION: Explicit in-text citations identifying a specific source.
Extract the ENTIRE citation block exactly as it appears in the tokens (e.g., 'Chomsky ( 1995 )', '[ 10 ]', '( Chan et al 2006 )').

8. SOURCES: The specific named entity or pronoun making a claim.
Extract the exact noun or noun phrase (e.g., 'Chomsky', 'researchers', 'Some studies', 'Dow Jones', 'His', 'the Scandinavian energy').
Tag SOURCES independently even when adjacent to ATTRIBUTION or CITATION — they are always separate spans.

9. ENDOPHORIC: Structural references to other parts of the same text.
Extract explicit document cross-references including the preposition if present (e.g., 'in Figure 1', 'Table 8', '( 11 )', 'All above', '( references )').

10. JUSTIFYING: Signals persuasion or substantiation — provides reasons, evidence, or causal explanation.
Extract causal clause introducers ('because', 'since', 'in that', 'due to') together with their dependent clause, or concluding transition words ('Thus', 'Therefore', 'Hence') with their clause.
